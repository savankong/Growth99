PE Acquisition Thesis

A buyer-side working plan: what a financial sponsor would diligence, and where the highest-conviction value-creation gaps sit. Analytical judgment layered on top of the researched facts above, not a new research pass — treat confidence tags in this section as strategic assessment, not sourced claims.

BLUF

Growth99 is a buy-it-messy-and-fix-it thesis, not a clean roll-up-ready SaaS asset. It has real distribution (1,000+ practices) in a fast-growing niche, but it is services-heavy, structurally missing the clinical/EMR layer that would make it sticky, and sitting under an unresolved six-plus-party search-fund cap table only 18 months into a CEO transition. The value isn't in what Growth99 already is — it's in (1) simplifying the ownership structure at a discount while it's still messy, (2) converting services delivery into AI-augmented margin rather than AI-as-marketing, and (3) using Growth99's existing scale to roll up fragmented point-solution competitors, starting with an EMR bolt-on to close its single biggest product gap. Don't pay a software multiple until the SaaS-vs-services mix is proven.

The thesis in one paragraph

Growth99 has something most of its point-solution competitors don't: scale and a marketing/lead-gen product genuinely differentiated from EMR-first rivals like AestheticsPro and PatientNow. What it lacks is proof it's a software company rather than an agency with a CRM bolted on, a cap table simple enough to transact cleanly, or a finished leadership story. The edge here isn't spotting the market — everyone can see ~14% CAGR med spa software growth — it's being willing to untangle ownership, re-price the services book, and buy the clinical capability the company is missing, while the business is still under-optimized enough that a strategic acquirer hasn't already bid the messiness away.

1 — What to look for in diligence

Revenue quality

Determines the multiple

Real SaaS-vs-managed-services split, by dollar and margin — Growth99's own pages describe hands-on SEO/ad delivery, which is agency economics, not software economics. NRR and gross churn segmented by practice size and tenure (single-location med spas skew SMB-churn, not enterprise). Pre- vs. post-CEO-transition cohort performance, not blended numbers — you're buying ~18–24 months of Pickell's track record.

Ownership & structure

Blocks the deal if unresolved

Full cap table map — at least six investor entities plus founder Cameron Hemphill, whose current status is unconfirmed anywhere public. Find out who actually holds a board seat vs. who's a passive LP before assuming "Pracinco and Trilogy" is the whole negotiation. Get Hemphill's exit terms and IP assignment in writing — an unresolved founder claim is a landmine in change-of-control.

Product & technology

Sets the multiple type

Get under the hood on Gia — no independent evidence it moves retention or conversion exists; everything public is Growth99's own marketing copy. Confirm how much of the core platform is built vs. licensed/white-labeled — the difference between a 70%-margin software company and a 40%-margin services company wearing a software UI.

Team & delivery

Churn predictor

CEO and CFO reference calls, plus Hemphill and any other pre-2024 departed execs — he's the only person with a clean pre/post comparison view. Account-manager/customer-facing turnover specifically — in a services-heavy delivery model this is closer to the real churn predictor than logo retention.

2 — Where the best gaps are to lean into

Ranked by expected value creation.

  1. Close the EMR/clinical gap — the highest-conviction lever. Every competitor comparison in this dossier (PatientNow, AestheticsPro) frames Growth99 the same way: strong on marketing, absent on clinical charting — the reason a practice needs a second vendor. Build or, more likely, bolt on a smaller EMR player. This converts Growth99 from "one tool in the stack" to "the system of record" — the move most likely to justify a re-rate at exit.
  2. Roll up the fragmented point-solution field. Boulevard, Mangomint, Vagaro, RepeatMD, AestheticsPro are all smaller, single-purpose tools. Growth99's 1,000+ practice base is real distribution scale most of them don't have — an unusually clean roll-up target list, since no single player yet has both marketing and clinical depth.
  3. Convert the services book into an AI margin story, not an AI marketing story. The more interesting use of the Gia-underlying AI investment isn't the customer-facing lead-response pitch — it's applying the same technology to cut the labor cost of the account-manager-dependent SEO/ad-management delivery. That's a direct COGS lever on the thinner-margin half of the revenue mix.
  4. Simplify the cap table while it's still cheap to do. A crowded search-fund syndicate this early post-recap (Oct 2024) likely has LPs who'd take liquidity at a modest premium rather than wait years for a search-fund-style outcome. Consolidating the smaller entities into a clean majority position is itself a value-creation move — buy complexity at a discount, sell simplicity at a premium later.
  5. Vertical adjacency expansion. Growth99's own content already spans dental and general medical marketing. Lower-conviction until real traction outside med spa is confirmed — treat as upside, not base case.
  6. Pricing and packaging. No public pricing exists and delivery looks bundled/ad hoc. A dedicated tiering/packaging pass (Gia as a paid add-on, usage-based AI monetization) is classic first-100-day playbook and likely low-hanging fruit.

3 — Deal structure to push for

Majority buyout / recap, not a minority check. A minority position alongside six other search-fund entities inherits the governance mess without the control to fix it — push for a structure that lets you consolidate or subordinate the smaller LPs.
Re-cut CEO incentives, don't assume continuity. Pickell's equity from the 2024 search-fund deal was built for a different ownership scenario — any new capital should come with a fresh alignment conversation.
Price the earnout/hold-back around Gia's unverified claims. Structure part of consideration contingent on Gia delivering measurable retention/conversion metrics post-close rather than paying full value for the AI story up front.

4 — What kills this thesis

If diligence shows the "SaaS" label is mostly aspirational and revenue is majority low-margin services, the multiple math doesn't work above a marketing-agency multiple. If Hemphill has an unresolved claim or the cap table can't be consolidated cleanly, transaction complexity may not be worth the discount. If churn among small single-location practices is running high (likely, unconfirmed), the "1,000+ practices" headline is a vanity metric, not a moat.

Recommended next step

Run a targeted pass against the four items that determine whether this thesis survives contact with real numbers: revenue-mix reconciliation, full cap-table mapping, Hemphill's current status, and an initial EMR bolt-on target list.

Executive Summary

Growth99 is no longer the company its brand history implies. Founder Cameron Hemphill grew it from a reputation-management tool into a 1,000+ practice marketing platform, but in October 2024 it was recapitalized via a search-fund acquisition — outside operator Rob Pickell became CEO, backed by at least six named investor entities. That transition, not the competitive product landscape, is the dominant fact of this diligence.

The Landscape

Growth99 sits inside the med spa / aesthetics / elective-wellness software market — every source found agrees it is growing fast, driven by injectables and aesthetic procedures becoming routine consumer spend. The reliable figures put the U.S. med-spa-software slice at roughly $119M (2023) growing toward ~$432M (2033), a 13.8% CAGR; a much larger figure ($950.5B) surfaced in one source and is flagged unreliable, likely a scoping error. Growth99 itself already claims 1,000+ practices and 2,300+ users — worth reconciling against real revenue once available.

Two competitive fronts

Point-solution software — Boulevard, Mangomint, PatientNow, RepeatMD, Vagaro, AestheticsPro, Tebra — none matched Growth99's top-of-funnel marketing depth, but several beat it on the capability it structurally lacks: clinical/EMR charting.

Traditional agencies — Rosemont Media, Etna Interactive, Brenton Way, Cardinal, Intrepy, Medstar, Influx, Studio 3 Marketing — identified but not yet profiled (see Method). Growth99's own marketing pitches directly against this front: "Better Than A Med Spa Digital Marketing Agency."

Unlike a typical VC-backed SaaS competitor set, Growth99 is not founder-led anymore. Ownership structure — not the product landscape — is likely the largest single risk factor in any new investment, because it directly determines preference stack, governance rights, and entry basis. See Ownership & Leadership.

The search-fund ownership model

Trend

Growth99 is a live example: acquired in 2024 via a traditional search fund, with Pickell backed by six-plus named investor entities rather than a single lead. Structurally distinct from VC/growth-equity SaaS — search funds acquire an already-profitable operating business and install an outside operator-CEO. This directly informs the preference-stack and governance questions: more distinct investor entities means a higher likelihood of a genuinely layered cap table.

Also bears on the primary-vs-secondary question: search-fund deals commonly give the operating entrepreneur founder-level equity while existing search-fund investors keep preferred positions — whether new capital would be primary growth capital or a partial LP buyout is not publicly disclosed.
