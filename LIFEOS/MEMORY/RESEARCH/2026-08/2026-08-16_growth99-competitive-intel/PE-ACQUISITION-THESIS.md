# PE Acquisition Thesis — Growth99

**Type:** Analytical judgment layered on top of the researched facts in this vault, not a new research pass. Confidence tags elsewhere in this vault are sourced claims; this document is strategic assessment and should be read as such.

## BLUF

Growth99 is a **buy-it-messy-and-fix-it** thesis, not a clean roll-up-ready SaaS asset. It has real distribution (1,000+ practices) in a fast-growing niche, but it is services-heavy, structurally missing the clinical/EMR layer that would make it sticky, and sitting under an unresolved six-plus-party search-fund cap table only 18 months into a CEO transition. The value isn't in what Growth99 already is — it's in (1) simplifying the ownership structure at a discount while it's still messy, (2) converting services delivery into AI-augmented margin rather than AI-as-marketing, and (3) using Growth99's existing scale to roll up fragmented point-solution competitors, starting with an EMR bolt-on to close its single biggest product gap. Don't pay a software multiple until the SaaS-vs-services mix is proven.

## The thesis in one paragraph

Growth99 has something most of its point-solution competitors don't: scale and a marketing/lead-gen product genuinely differentiated from EMR-first rivals like AestheticsPro and PatientNow (see [Companies/AestheticsPro.md](Companies/AestheticsPro.md), [Companies/PatientNow.md](Companies/PatientNow.md)). What it lacks is proof it's a software company rather than an agency with a CRM bolted on, a cap table simple enough to transact cleanly, or a finished leadership story. The edge here isn't spotting the market — everyone can see ~14% CAGR med spa software growth (see [Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md](Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md)) — it's being willing to untangle ownership, re-price the services book, and buy the clinical capability the company is missing, while the business is still under-optimized enough that a strategic acquirer hasn't already bid the messiness away.

## 1 — What to look for in diligence

**Revenue quality (determines the multiple).** Real SaaS-vs-managed-services split, by dollar and margin — Growth99's own pages describe hands-on SEO/ad delivery, which is agency economics, not software economics (see the mix conflict noted in [Companies/Growth99.md](Companies/Growth99.md)). NRR and gross churn segmented by practice size and tenure. Pre- vs. post-CEO-transition cohort performance, not blended numbers.

**Ownership & structure (blocks the deal if unresolved).** Full cap table map — at least six investor entities plus founder Cameron Hemphill, whose current status is unconfirmed anywhere public (see [People/Cameron-Hemphill.md](People/Cameron-Hemphill.md)). Find out who holds a board seat vs. who's a passive LP. Get Hemphill's exit terms and IP assignment in writing.

**Product & technology (sets the multiple type).** Get under the hood on Gia — no independent evidence it moves retention or conversion exists (see [Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md](Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md)). Confirm how much of the core platform is built vs. licensed/white-labeled.

**Team & delivery (churn predictor).** CEO and CFO reference calls, plus Hemphill and any other pre-2024 departed execs. Account-manager/customer-facing turnover specifically.

## 2 — Where the best gaps are to lean into (ranked)

1. **Close the EMR/clinical gap — the highest-conviction lever.** Every competitor comparison in this vault frames Growth99 the same way: strong on marketing, absent on clinical charting (see [Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md](Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md)). Build or bolt on a smaller EMR player to become the system of record.
2. **Roll up the fragmented point-solution field.** Boulevard, Mangomint, Vagaro, RepeatMD, AestheticsPro are all smaller, single-purpose tools. Growth99's 1,000+ practice base is real distribution scale most of them don't have.
3. **Convert the services book into an AI margin story, not an AI marketing story.** Apply the Gia-underlying AI investment to cut the labor cost of account-manager-dependent SEO/ad-management delivery — a direct COGS lever on the thinner-margin half of revenue.
4. **Simplify the cap table while it's still cheap to do.** A crowded search-fund syndicate this early post-recap likely has LPs who'd take liquidity at a modest premium (see [Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md](Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md)).
5. **Vertical adjacency expansion** (dental, general medical) — lower conviction until real traction is confirmed; treat as upside.
6. **Pricing and packaging.** No public pricing exists; a dedicated tiering pass (Gia as paid add-on, usage-based AI monetization) is likely low-hanging fruit.

## 3 — Deal structure to push for

- **Majority buyout / recap, not a minority check.** A minority position alongside six other search-fund entities inherits the governance mess without the control to fix it.
- **Re-cut CEO incentives, don't assume continuity.** Pickell's equity from the 2024 search-fund deal was built for a different ownership scenario.
- **Price the earnout/hold-back around Gia's unverified claims.** Structure part of consideration contingent on Gia delivering measurable metrics post-close.

## 4 — What kills this thesis

If diligence shows the "SaaS" label is mostly aspirational and revenue is majority low-margin services, the multiple math doesn't work above a marketing-agency multiple. If Hemphill has an unresolved claim or the cap table can't be consolidated cleanly, transaction complexity may not be worth the discount. If churn among small single-location practices is running high (likely, unconfirmed), the "1,000+ practices" headline is a vanity metric, not a moat.

## Recommended next step

Run a targeted pass against the four items that determine whether this thesis survives contact with real numbers: revenue-mix reconciliation, full cap-table mapping, Hemphill's current status, and an initial EMR bolt-on target list.
