# Exit Readiness Plan — Positioning Growth99 for PE Acquisition in 12–24 Months

**Reframed pass 2, 2026-08-16.** The original version of this document (pass 1) analyzed Growth99 from a PE **buyer's** diligence perspective. This version reorients every finding toward the opposite goal: **getting Growth99 acquired by a PE firm within 12–24 months**, at the best achievable multiple. Facts are the same underlying research; the strategic lens has flipped from "where would I as a buyer press for a discount" to "what does the seller need to fix, prove, or build before a buyer shows up." Analytical judgment layered on top of researched facts — confidence tags on individual claims are sourced; the roadmap itself is strategic judgment.

## BLUF

Growth99 can plausibly sell to a PE firm within 12–24 months, but not as-is — three things currently suppress the achievable multiple, and all three are fixable on that timeline. **(1)** The revenue-mix and unit-economics story is unproven publicly and probably unproven internally in a buyer-ready format — get NRR/churn/CAC cohorts and the SaaS-vs-services split clean and documented now. **(2)** The cap table is a real transaction-risk: pass 2 confirms no public securities trail exists (SEC Form D searches for "Growth99," "Growth99 Holdings," and "Pracinco" all return zero hits), meaning the 6–8-entity search-fund syndicate's preference stack and consent rights are opaque even to us — a buyer will demand this get simplified before signing, so start now rather than during a live process. **(3)** The single highest-leverage move available is closing the EMR/clinical-charting gap — Growth99's most consistently cited weakness — via a real acquisition; **Symplast** (self-funded, right specialty, ~$8M ARR range, no competing PE owner) is the strongest identified candidate and is realistically closeable inside this window. One major uncertainty from pass 1 has now resolved in the company's favor at no cost: founder **Cameron Hemphill's exit is confirmed clean** — he's COO at an unrelated firm (Terri Ross Consulting) as of March 2026 — removing a founder-overhang red flag before any buyer even asks about it.

## The 12–24 month roadmap

### Phase 1 (Months 0–6): Make the numbers buyer-legible
The single biggest risk to any future sale isn't a bad number — it's an unprovable one. A buyer's diligence team doing what this vault's research just did will hit the same wall: no public revenue mix, no public NRR/churn, no public cap table. That's normal for a private company, but it means the *first* thing a real process will ask for is a clean data room, and that takes real internal work to assemble, not just a document dump.
- **Instrument and report the SaaS-vs-managed-services split** by dollar and by margin, monthly, starting now — so 12–24 months from now there's a trend line, not a single retroactive estimate. This single number is the difference between a software multiple and an agency multiple at exit (see [Companies/Growth99.md](Companies/Growth99.md) for why this matters).
- **Build NRR/gross-churn cohorts segmented by practice size and tenure**, and separately, **pre- vs. post-October-2024-CEO-transition cohorts.** A buyer will build this themselves if the company doesn't — better to own the narrative with real data.
- **Simplify the cap table.** Pass 2 confirms this is not a paperwork nicety: with zero public securities-filing trail and 6–8 named investor entities, a future buyer's counsel will need every consent right mapped before close. Start consolidating smaller search-fund LPs (Search Fund Partners, Relay Investments, Endurance Search Partners, and any unconfirmed remainder) into a cleaner structure now, while there's no deal deadline forcing a bad price on that cleanup. See [Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md](Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md).
- **No action needed on Hemphill.** Confirmed clean exit — see [People/Cameron-Hemphill.md](People/Cameron-Hemphill.md). Keep a light watch only because his new role (COO at a med-spa-adjacent consultancy) sits in an adjacent space, not because of any transaction risk.

### Phase 2 (Months 4–14, overlapping Phase 1): Close the EMR gap and prove the AI story
This phase is the actual value-creation engine — the difference between selling as "a marketing agency with software" and selling as "the platform of record for aesthetic practices."
- **Pursue Symplast as an acquisition.** See [Companies/Symplast.md](Companies/Symplast.md) for the full profile. It is the strongest bolt-on candidate found: specialty-matched (cosmetic surgery/med spa EMR), realistically sized (~$8M ARR range against Growth99's own ~$25–50M estimated revenue), and — critically — has no competing institutional PE owner to negotiate around, unlike Meevo (Serent Capital-backed) or Nextech (TPG-owned). There is no evidence Symplast is currently for sale; this requires proactive, direct outreach, not waiting for a process. Closing this deal converts Growth99 from "one tool in the stack" to "the system of record" — the single most repeated gap called out across every competitor comparison in this vault (see [Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md](Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md)).
- **Get real, measurable data on Gia's impact — not marketing copy.** Every public claim about Gia's retention/conversion impact traces back to Growth99's own marketing (see [Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md](Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md)). A buyer's diligence team will discount an unproven AI story to zero, the same way this vault did. Twelve to eighteen months of real before/after cohort data — response time, conversion rate, retention lift, ideally per-practice — converts "marketing language" into a defensible part of the multiple.
- **Use the AI investment to cut services COGS, not just to pitch customers.** Applying the same technology to reduce the labor cost of account-manager-dependent SEO/ad-management delivery is a direct, provable margin story for a buyer — arguably more convincing than the customer-facing pitch, because it shows up in gross margin, not just marketing copy.

### Phase 3 (Months 14–20): Build the equity story and engage the market
- **Engage a sell-side advisor/banker** with vertical-SaaS or healthcare-marketing-tech experience 6–9 months ahead of a target close, once Phases 1–2 have real data behind them — going to market too early, before the revenue-mix and EMR stories are provable, risks anchoring buyers to an agency-multiple read that's hard to walk back later in the same process.
- **Build the buyer list deliberately.** Likely categories: (a) larger practice-management/EHR platforms looking to add marketing-automation capability (the Tebra/PatientPop precedent in this vault is a cautionary tale on post-merger integration quality, not a reason to avoid this buyer type — see [Companies/Tebra-PatientPop.md](Companies/Tebra-PatientPop.md)), (b) healthcare-vertical-focused PE funds already active in med spa roll-ups (see [Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md](Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md) for the growth backdrop that makes this category attractive), (c) horizontal marketing-automation platforms seeking a healthcare vertical beachhead.
- **Pre-empt the two-fronts question.** Any buyer will ask "how do you win against point-solution CRMs and against agencies simultaneously" (see [LANDSCAPE.md](LANDSCAPE.md)) — have a crisp, evidenced answer ready rather than fielding it live for the first time in a management presentation.

### Phase 4 (Months 20–24): Run the process
- With clean financials, a simplified cap table, a closed EMR acquisition, and real AI-impact data in hand, run a competitive process rather than a single-bidder negotiation — the entire point of Phases 1–3 is to earn a software-multiple outcome instead of an agency-multiple one, and that leverage only shows up with real competing bids.
- Structure the process to let the existing search-fund investors (Pracinco, Trilogy, Hunter Search Capital, and the smaller LPs) exit cleanly alongside management — a well-sequenced process should resolve the cap-table complexity as part of closing, not before it, once a buyer's price is locked in.

## What could blow the 12–24 month timeline

- **If the SaaS-vs-services split turns out to be mostly services once actually measured**, Phase 2's EMR acquisition still helps, but the achievable multiple compresses regardless — plan messaging around a realistic blended multiple rather than a pure-SaaS comp from the outset.
- **If Symplast (or an equivalent target) isn't willing to sell**, the EMR gap has to be built organically instead, which is slower and less certain to land inside 24 months — worth quietly scoping a build-vs-buy fallback in Phase 1, not after a Symplast approach fails.
- **If cap-table consolidation drags** — search-fund LPs can be slow to agree to consolidation without a live deal forcing the question — Phase 1's cleanup may need to run in parallel with early buyer conversations rather than fully precede them.
