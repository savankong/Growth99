# Search Fund Ownership Model in Vertical SaaS/Marketing Tech

**Status:** RESEARCHED
**Category:** Trend — Ownership/Governance Structure
**Value:** CRITICAL

## Overview

- Growth99 itself is a live example of this trend: acquired/recapitalized in 2024 via a **traditional search fund** structure, with entrepreneur Rob Pickell backed by a syndicate of at least six named search-fund-focused investor entities (Trilogy Search Partners, Pracinco, Hunter Search Capital, Search Fund Partners, Relay Investments, Endurance Search Partners, and possibly Cambria Group). [HIGH — directly evidenced by this specific deal] — [Searcher Insights — Growth99 deal page](https://searcherinsights.com/network/deals/growth99/), [Hunter Search Capital](https://www.huntersearchcapital.com/portfolio-companies/growth99)
- This is structurally distinct from a traditional VC/growth-equity-backed SaaS company: search funds acquire an already-profitable, already-operating business and install an outside operator-CEO, rather than funding a founder-led company through growth stages. This has direct implications for the diligence questions about preference stack and governance — a **multi-investor search-fund syndicate** (6+ named entities) typically means a correspondingly complex cap table with multiple parties holding preferred stock and protective provisions, not a single clean lead investor. [HIGH — structural inference from the confirmed investor list]

## Relevance to Diligence

- Directly informs the brief's questions about "preferred stock stack, liquidation preferences, and existing board seats" — the number of distinct investor entities involved (6+) raises the likelihood of a genuinely complex stack that needs careful legal review before any new capital enters.
- Also informs the "primary vs. secondary" deal-structure question: search fund deals commonly involve the operating entrepreneur (Pickell) receiving founder-level equity while existing search-fund investors retain preferred positions — a new investor's capital could plausibly be either growth primary capital or a partial buyout of specific search-fund LPs, and this is not publicly disclosed.

## Open Questions for Next Pass

- Confirm which of the 6 named investor entities actually hold board seats vs. passive economic stakes (search funds often have 1-2 board-level investors and several passive LPs).
