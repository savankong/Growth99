# LANDSCAPE — Growth99 in the Med Spa / Aesthetics MarTech Market

## The Market

Growth99 sits inside the medical spa / aesthetics / elective wellness software market, which every source found agrees is growing fast (mid-to-high teens CAGR), driven by injectables and aesthetic procedures becoming routine consumer spend rather than niche medical care. See [Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md](Trends/Med-Spa-Market-Growth.md) — the reliable figures put the U.S. med-spa-**software** slice at roughly $119M (2023) growing toward ~$432M (2033); one much larger figure ($950.5B) was found and flagged as an unreliable outlier, likely a scoping error in that source.

Growth99 itself already claims 1,000+ practices and 2,300+ users — a meaningful share of a market this size, which is worth reconciling against real revenue figures once available.

## Two Competitive Fronts

The diligence brief correctly frames Growth99 as fighting on two fronts, and this pass's research confirms that framing:

**Front 1 — Point-solution CRM/practice-management software.** Profiled: Boulevard (booking + texting, salon-adapted), Mangomint (scheduling/team management), PatientNow (CRM + EMR), RepeatMD (loyalty/financing add-on), Vagaro (budget/entry-level), AestheticsPro (EMR/compliance-first), Tebra/PatientPop (broad healthcare practice management, post-merger reputation risk). None of these were found to match Growth99's top-of-funnel marketing depth (SEO, paid ads, lead gen); several instead beat Growth99 on the capability Growth99 structurally lacks — **clinical/EMR charting**. See [Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md](Technologies/EMR-Clinical-Charting.md).

**Front 2 — Traditional marketing agencies.** Identified but not yet profiled in depth (PENDING in ENTITIES.md): Rosemont Media, Etna Interactive, Brenton Way, Cardinal, Intrepy, Medstar, Influx, Studio 3 Marketing. These compete on service quality and creative/brand work rather than integrated software — Growth99's pitch against them is "platform, not agency" (a phrase that appears directly in Growth99's own marketing: "Better Than A Med Spa Digital Marketing Agency").

## Growth99's Differentiation Bet: AI (Gia)

Growth99's most visible current strategic bet is **Gia**, an AI front-desk/lead-response agent launched as part of the October 2024 relaunch under new CEO Rob Pickell (see [Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md](Products/Gia-AI-Front-Desk.md)). This pass found no directly comparable AI-lead-response feature publicly marketed by any of the 7 point-solution competitors profiled — a potential near-term moat — but this was not exhaustively verified per-competitor and is flagged as the single highest-priority next-pass item (see [Trends/AI-Native-Lead-Engagement-Arms-Race.md](Trends/AI-Native-Lead-Engagement-Arms-Race.md)). Independent evidence of Gia's actual retention/conversion impact (as opposed to Growth99's own marketing claims) was not found.

## Ownership Structure Is the Landscape's Defining Feature

Unlike a typical VC-backed SaaS competitor set, Growth99 itself is not founder-led anymore — it was recapitalized in 2024 via a **search fund** acquisition (see [Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md](Trends/Search-Fund-Roll-Up-Model.md)). At least six named investor entities are involved (Pracinco, Trilogy Search Partners, Hunter Search Capital, Search Fund Partners, Relay Investments, Endurance Search Partners, possibly Cambria Group), and operating CEO Rob Pickell was installed from outside rather than promoted from within. Founder Cameron Hemphill is fully separated from the company — confirmed via his subsequent full-time role at an unrelated firm — with no ongoing tie. Ownership structure looked like it might be the largest risk factor in any new investment; instead, both major ownership questions have resolved cleanly (see [PE-ACQUISITION-THESIS.md](PE-ACQUISITION-THESIS.md) for the cap-table detail), leaving the real work on the operating side.

## What This Pass Could Not Determine (By Design, Not Oversight)

Per the research method's "never fabricate" instruction, the following categories were deliberately left unfilled because no public source disclosed them — they require direct management/investor engagement, not public web research:
- Revenue, revenue mix (SaaS vs. services), NRR, gross churn, customer concentration
- CAC and payback period trends
- Preferred stock stack, liquidation preferences, board composition
- Employee turnover in customer-facing roles
- Pre/post-CEO-transition cohort performance data
